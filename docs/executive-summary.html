Why did 0.2778 win? — byte-level autopsy (verified this session)

1. It is an emission optimum, not a new detection. Decoded pixels prove exact nesting: the champion's 37,654 dots are a 100% subset of GEMSDOE31's best (40,199), itself a subset of the D2.8 raster (44,090; GEMSDOE25 ≡ GEMSDOE30 pixel-identical). GEMSDOE36 (0.2750) is the champion plus 1,200 dots. One H19-5 ridge surface; only dot placement differs.

2. Spacing ≈ kernel radius. DTI credits each hidden fault pixel by the maximum kernel-weighted prediction within 300 m (3 px), with α=0.2 (FP) vs β=0.8 (FN). Dots closer than ~3 px duplicate credit while each still risks FP cost. Median NN spacing measures 2.24 px on the D1.5 files (0.2449–0.2477 at ~61k dots) vs 3.0 px on D2.8 (0.2600–0.2710 at 40–44k).

3. B=2 catalogue-flank prune. DrivenData staff confirm known-fault pixels are masked/excluded from scoring (community post 11516, chrisk-dd). Dots 1–2 px off a known fault are still scored and almost surely FP (expected credit below the DTI marginal bar α·DTI ≈ 0.056 at 0.2778). Measured near-catalogue dot share tracks the ladder exactly: 14.6% (0.2600) → 6.3% (0.2708) → 0.0% (0.2778; 2,545 dots deleted).

4. Holdout trap warning. Scoring those FIXED masks on withheld catalogue segments anti-ranks the live ladder (0.0992 > 0.0301 > 0.0045, HOLDOUT-DTI v3, 22,232 withheld): B-gated masks cannot hit segments they were gated away from. Any lane tuning final masks on that evaluator will un-learn B-pruning. All gating here therefore uses fold-local emission.
