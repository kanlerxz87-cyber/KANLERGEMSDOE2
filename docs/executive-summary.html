Why GEMSDOE32’s 0.2778 is not a submission target

Forensic read of the highest reported score

Why it scored (plausible mechanism)

GEMSDOE32’s mask equals GEMSDOE25’s after removing ~6,436 dots within two pixels of mirrored known faults. Pruning redundant, catalogue-adjacent emitted mass is a plausible precision play, and the dotted-h19-5 family places dots on/near mapped faults where hidden truth is concentrated. That explains a high leaderboard score—but the dots are shared across GEMSDOE24/25/27/28/30/31/32/33/36, so the file is not unique under the literal all-registry rule (>70% within 3 px of a registered raster).

Why we cannot replicate or beat it from local evidence

No hidden labels, file-specific organizer receipt, or original scoring replay is available locally. The official DrivenData training data require login, which this sandbox does not have. A catalogued-fault proximity surface scores holdout DTI 0.3795 in our hide-and-recover holdout, but its leakage-canary AUC is 1.00—it reads withheld fault locations directly, so that score is leakage, not discovery. Our compliant catalogue-independent geophysical surface scores HOLDOUT-DTI 0.009154 (CI [0.006528, 0.012243], leakage AUC 0.5910): honest but modest, and that is the estimate we stand behind.

How to submit (once cleared)

DrivenData submission instructions

  1. Clear every gate first. Fold-visible-only features, per-feature leakage canaries (≤0.90 AUC), a comparable holdout win with 95% CI, literal complete-registry checks on surface and dots (≤0.90 rank correlation, ≤0.70 dot overlap), and current account weekly quota. An independent selector must approve the exact SHA-256.
  2. Download the exact approved TIFF. Verify SHA-256, one float32 band, CRS EPSG:32611, shape 3730×3292, 100 m geotransform, and in-footprint [0,1] values against the authenticated sample.
  3. Open DrivenData → Submit → Make new submission. Upload the single-band GeoTIFF (or a ZIP containing only that TIFF). Enter the unique name and a short note (≤140 characters).
  4. Preserve the organizer receipt. Record file hash, timestamp, and score only if returned by the organizer. Choose one final entry for both rounds before the deadline.

The [0,1] submission error

The portal rejects values outside [0,1]; NaN comparisons fail that check. Our candidate uses zeros outside the footprint so ((a>=0)&(a<=1)).all() holds over the whole array—this is the encoding that avoids the Predicted values must be in range [0, 1] error. The official text says null/NaN outside bounds, but portal acceptance of either exterior convention is not established by this sandbox; verify against the current authenticated sample before upload.
