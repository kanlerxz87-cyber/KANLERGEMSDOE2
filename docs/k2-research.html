Research notes

Think beyond the map.

A preregistered single lane, a rigorous stop, and source-linked next steps for genuinely unmapped structure.

Negative result · no experiment promoted

The geometric feasibility test stopped the selected K2-SCALE lane before the predictive surface or candidate existed.

The lattice makes this budget impossible

Only 6,649 cells in the pinned owner-mirror footprint lie more than three pixels from the registered lattice. At a fixed budget of 37,654 unique dots, at least 31,005 must be within its halo. The lower bound is 82.34%, exceeding the protocol's 70% stop. It is a lower bound on any possible arrangement, not a measurement of a candidate.

A smaller budget could satisfy this one necessary condition, but that would be a new design and would still need every other gate. This stopped lane was not retuned.

A parallel artifact is also a duplicate

An independently produced E1 raster was already quarantined on the evolving main branch. We re-opened its exact bytes: 37,395 of its 37,654 dots (99.31%) fall within three pixels of the same registered lattice. The earlier nine-file E1 comparison excluded that lattice. This one-raster check is a duplicate stop, not a new experiment or a surface-correlation audit. Open the retrospective receipt →

The selected hypothesis

K2-SCALE proposes that a concealed boundary may create colocated magnetic and topographic gradients with a relatively stable cross-scale curvature phase. A lithologic contact, volcanic flow edge, or processing artifact can mimic this signature. No result supports the hypothesis yet; it does not establish geothermal vent locations.

Three alternatives, not experiments

The preregistered slate also ranks cross-scarp drainage offsets, paleo-thermal deposits plus shallow probes, and multiscale magnetic–gravity junctions. The first two need specific official data products not directly obtainable in this sandbox; the last has substantial overlap risk with earlier work. None was substituted after the stop.
