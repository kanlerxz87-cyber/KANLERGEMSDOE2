## How the reported GEMSDOE32 result may have arisen

The owner's 0.2778 report has **no organizer receipt linked to the raster's hash** in the reviewed material. The reproducible [H33 lineage audit](../evidence/h33_lineage_20261026.json) compares exact pinned peer rasters: the selected GEMSDOE31 file has 40,199 dots; GEMSDOE32 has 37,654. GEMSDOE32 removes exactly the 2,545 GEMSDOE31 dots within two Euclidean pixels of the peer-mirrored known-fault catalogue, and adds none. The official triangular kernel gives partial credit for nearby predictions, while the Tversky penalties weigh missed truth more than false positives. Consequently a judiciously spaced, sparse support can cover more distinct truth than redundant dots for a similar false-positive mass. Masking low-credit known-fault flanks may further change that trade-off. These are plausible mechanisms, not a causal demonstration: hidden labels, true organizer scoring inputs, and score-to-hash receipts for the two rasters are unavailable. The official task asks for predictions of all faults, so simply zeroing known faults should not be treated as proven optimal for final-round evaluation.

The stronger result is not evidence that a fresh candidate will exceed it. Nor does a high public leaderboard value guarantee a high private or final-round value. No defensible conversion from the local, imperfect catalogue holdout to an organizer score has been calibrated here.
