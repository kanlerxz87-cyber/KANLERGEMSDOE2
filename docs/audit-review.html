Next steps

  1. Decide which instrument governs promotion. One option is to submit ONE adjudication file, which the protocol currently blocks because no file passes uniqueness. The other is to amend the protocol so that a leave-site-out-validated live-ledger projection, with its error band, can serve as a gate.
  2. Ask the organizers, or watch forums 11563/11584, whether NBMG CC BY 4.0 layers (Quaternary faults, 1:500k geology on the ArcGIS Hub) and USGS-published quadrangle fault plates may be used. Detailed published fault maps are the only identified source of information absent from every registry raster.
  3. If allowed, build a fault-map-distance factor and refit the LLI. H1 says it must improve the leave-site-out MAE by at least 0.001 before any emission.
  4. Train a 1 m LiDAR scarp detector on catalogue scarps (USGS 3DEP 1 m DEM, public domain; not reachable from this sandbox). The 100 m aggregated scarp features carry less than 0.001 of extra live-ledger signal.
  5. Re-audit the H19-5 provenance (GEMSDOE19) for catalogue-label use before it serves as any holdout feature.

Limitations

  • Every live score is USER-REPORTED, with no organizer receipt.
  • LLI projections carry about ±0.025 leave-site-out MAE. On the nine focus files they are biased high by +0.012 on average, and an optimiser-curse bias applies to any candidate emitted against the same model.
  • The registry check covers 360 registry rasters and 124 ledger rasters, not every raster in existence.
  • HIDE-RECOVER and SGMC-CAL measure catalogue-like faults, not the expert-mapped scored population.
