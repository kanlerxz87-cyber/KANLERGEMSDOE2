Why did 0.2778 win, and can we beat it?

Mechanism (PhD autopsy). All nine bests are the same H19-5 multiline-corroborated ridge surface; only emission differs. (1) Dot spacing ≈ kernel: DTI uses a 300 m triangular kernel (3 px), α=0.2 (FP) vs β=0.8 (FN) — missing a fault costs 4× a wasted dot, but dots closer than ~3 px give redundant kernel credit while each still risks FP cost. Densities confirm it: D1.5 (~61k dots) scored 0.2449–0.2477 < D2.8 (40–44k) 0.2600–0.2710. (2) B=2 catalogue-flank prune: deleting every dot within 2 px of known faults (40,199→37,654) raised 0.2708→0.2778. Near-catalogue dots have expected credit below the marginal bar α·DTI≈0.056: known faults are masked in scoring, mapping is most complete there, and blind faults are continuations away from mapped traces. (3) Nothing outside this lineage in the prompt list exceeded 0.24, so surface novelty alone has not paid — emission geometry has.

Can we beat 0.2778? Not with evidence in hand. Our genuinely different E1 surface beats the champion on catalogue hide-recover (0.0251 vs 0.0045, same vendored evaluator, 22,232 withheld) and on quadrant OOF (0.1252), but trails on the only blind-fault proxy available locally (SGMC, 0.0752 vs 0.0961). The SGMC-anchored estimate is ~0.22. Claiming >0.2778 would require the catalogue-recovery proxy to dominate the blind proxy — upstream repos that made that assumption produced the string of 0.02–0.16 live scores in the prompt list. We therefore do not promote E1.
