Why the previous E1 result was stopped

The corrected, shared Euclidean check finds 99.31% of E1 dots within 3 pixels of an included calibration lattice, above the literal 70% stop. Its previous audit excluded that lattice and used Manhattan rather than Euclidean dilation. E1's pre-placement surface is unavailable, so its surface uniqueness cannot be reconstructed.

The prior quadrant split did not hide whole connected fault components, and its fixed-mask analysis used a model trained on the full catalogue. Its holdout and projected leaderboard claims are therefore withdrawn. Read the corrected audit →

The lattice covers 99.87% of the footprint within 3 pixels. Any raster with at least 22,164 positive pixels necessarily exceeds the 70% rule against it; this is a geometric conflict in the protocol, not proof of geological copying. We do not exempt the lattice or reposition dots to game the test.

Why the reported GEMSDOE32 result may have led

RASTER-AUDIT: the selected GEMSDOE32 H33 B=2 raster is exactly the selected GEMSDOE31 raster after removing 2,545 of 40,199 dots within two Euclidean pixels of the peer-mirrored known catalogue; no dots were added. Inspect the lineage audit →

The official distance-tolerant metric can reward nonredundant coverage while penalizing false-positive mass, making this a plausible mechanism—not a causal proof. The exact score-to-file receipts and hidden truth are unavailable, and the competition asks for predictions of all faults. No result here establishes that a new strategy will beat the reported value or the current public leader.
