Why the dotted champion may work

The pinned GEMSDOE32 source snapshot describes H33-2-B2 as a dotted ridge-derived mask, with catalogue-near dots pruned. The official DTI gives false negatives four times the alpha-weighted false-positive coefficient (beta 0.8 vs alpha 0.2) and triangular distance credit within 300 m. A sparse, spatially broad ridge mask can plausibly retain credit around hidden faults while limiting false-positive prediction mass; pruning around known faults may reduce direct dependence on the catalogue. Pinned site records describe a nested H19-5 → D2.8 → B1 → B2 family, with dots pruned near mapped faults; the historical scores remain owner-reported claims. The mechanism is plausible because low-marginal-credit dots near known traces may be removed while preserving distance-kernel coverage elsewhere, but only the exact scored TIFF and hidden labels could establish causation. Also, the `h33-h33-2-b2` filename does not by itself prove it came from the later H33-A..E experimental module in the archived source tree. See the detailed audit in the project README and the source irregularity log. No higher leaderboard score can be inferred from this chain alone.
