What the strongest claim tells us

The largest owner-reported historical value in the nine-site prompt is GEMSDOE32 B2, 0.2778. No organizer submission receipt linking that number to the identified bytes was available. The pinned B2 raster is exactly the older D2.8 mask after removing dots at or within two pixels of the visible catalogue.

It is scientifically plausible that reducing false-positive mass improved the distance-weighted Tversky index. But the organizer's hidden truth is unavailable, and the lost true-positive credit is unknown. New faults may also lie within 300 m of known faults. Therefore we cannot prove the reason for the claimed change, or infer a reliable leaderboard score for a new file.

How the comparison was made

All nine named files were fetched at immutable Git commits, verified by SHA-256 and compared on a hash-pinned owner-mirror footprint. The uniqueness column uses a directional 3-pixel overlap against the other eight historical focus files; it does not cover the original prediction surfaces or all concurrent registry rasters. Owner-mirror integrity is not organizer authentication.

GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 have identical prediction values inside the footprint, even though their TIFF bytes differ. GEMSDOE36 contains the entire GEMSDOE32 B2 set plus 1,200 dots. Renaming, zero-filling, or re-encoding these historical files is not a new scientific submission.
