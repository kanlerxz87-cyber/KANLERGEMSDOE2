1 Cross-scarp radiometric texture displacement. Peer-cached GeoDAWN K, Th and U plus 3DEP-derived LiDAR scarp orientation. Compare texture patches on opposite sides of a scarp for a consistent signed along-strike offset in multiple channels and scales. An offset in shallow surface material may survive where the displacement itself is subdued or unmapped. No catalogue location would enter the physical transform. Sedimentary facies contacts, erosion, and flight-line/seam artifacts. Require agreement across independently supported channels, coverage masks, acquisition-block controls and a gradient-only ablation. Not the local E1 magnetic/gravity HGB. A peer TDX study already uses related magnetic profile correspondence; this proposal uses radiometrics and scarp orientation, so conceptual overlap is substantial. Promising but uncertain / medium-high cost. Quantization may destroy the texture needed.
2 Along-strike polarity reversals. Reduced-to-pole magnetic data or upward-continued magnetic grids plus LiDAR scarp orientation; test persistent changes in signed across-scarp magnetic step along a trace, instead of ranking edge magnitude alone. Changes in magnetization across a buried fault may extend beyond visible mapped traces. Remanent magnetization, intrusive/lithologic boundaries and survey seams; require continuous topographic support and acquisition-boundary controls. Different from E1's scalar cross-gradient. Similar signed-lag continuity was proposed in the peer texture-controls plan; do not represent it as globally novel. Moderate, uncertain / medium cost.
3 Drainage-line displacement. Official USGS 3DEP bare-earth elevation and a new flow-network derivation; seek repeated signed offsets of several channels across a candidate line. Consistent channel offsets could expose a through-going structure that is missed where surface scarps are weak. Stream capture, lithologic barriers, roads and canals; test upstream/downstream pairs and infrastructure masks. Tests coherent displacement rather than E1's LiDAR dipole amplitude. High cost; not yet executable here: raw DEM tiles and a verified hydrologic preprocessing pipeline are absent.
4 Paired alteration shoulders. Peer-cached GeoDAWN K, Th, U and magnetic field; test a pair of narrow, spatially consistent radiometric shoulders flanking a magnetic step. Concealed structural permeability and alteration may produce paired cross-profile patterns even without a mapped scarp. Weathering, transported sediments, natural lithologic zoning; require support masks, lithologic controls and survey-boundary ablation. Explicit shoulder symmetry rather than E1's unconstrained cross-gradient. Other peer alteration/flank methods exist; novelty unresolved. Uncertain / medium cost.

Data feasibility, provenance and limits

  • The organizer problem description identifies the fault target, supplied geophysical features and conditional use of licensed external data. The organizer reference implementation explicitly provides a CPU option. A GPU is not an absolute requirement.
  • USGS GeoDAWN identifies the data release, survey blocks, flight specifications, processing and CC0 rights. The linked GeoTIFF and flight-line products are described on the official page. The locally restored uint8 files are peer-derived, not verified as independently reconstructed from those raw data.
  • The USGS 3DEP page states that 3DEP products are free and without use restrictions and provides access tools. Actual required tiles and their complete study-area coverage have not been retrieved or verified in this sandbox. The organizer's DEM link list is behind login.
  • The DOE Geothermal Data Repository INGENIOUS compilation describes publicly accessible fault, thermal, well, spring, seismic and other layers under CC BY 4.0; use requires attribution and checking each linked resource. Do not include catalogue-derived columns in fold-independent features. Location and temperature sampling are biased toward where people looked.
  • Core GeoTIFF mirrors and three derived caches now match pinned peer SHA-256 and the mirrored sample grid; see data_readiness.json. A hash verifies equality to that mirror, not its official origin, processing quality, licensing or generalization.

Experiment protocol once the stop rule is resolved

Preassign one exclusive lane and freeze the complete registry, cached feature lineage, current best and folds. Test each actual feature alone on withheld positives; AUC above 0.90 is a leakage alarm and a lower AUC does not prove independence. Hide whole connected fault components and a buffer; derive catalogue features from visible faults only; mask visible fault pixels exactly. Use the shared SegmentHoldout for pooled distance-weighted Tversky components, a spatial-bootstrap 95% CI and a paired comparison with an independently reconstructed best on identical folds. Check the surface before placement, then final dots, against all registry entries without exemptions. Stop at the first duplicate; never jitter an old file to evade the rule. Use at most three experiments or two hours, and do not spend a competition slot without the separate selector and authenticated allowance check.
