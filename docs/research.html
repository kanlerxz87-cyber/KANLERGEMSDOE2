Better evidence, not a louder score.

Our objective is to map previously unknown faults that may indicate geothermal resources. A heat anomaly or vent is not itself a confirmed fault.

Forensic analysis

What explains the reported 0.2778?

What the bytes show

The GEMSDOE32 raster is exactly the GEMSDOE25 dotted raster after removing pixels ≤2 px from the restored known-fault mask: XOR = 0; 6,436 pixels are removed. The GEMSDOE36 raster contains every GEMSDOE32 dot plus 1,200 more.

GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 have identical in-footprint positive masks despite different file bytes. All nine historical leaders exceed the 70% overlap threshold against at least one other focus-site raster.

What remains a hypothesis

Under distance-weighted Tversky, thinning redundant predictions or removing locations with little hidden-truth credit can reduce false-positive cost while retaining recall. This is a plausible explanation for the reported improvement, not a measured causal decomposition.

Hidden expert truth, an organizer receipt linking the 0.2778 score to bytes, and the original experiment replay are unavailable. An earlier reciprocal-score fit cannot prove zero true positives, infer hidden truth counts, or establish a score ceiling.

Ranked before modeling

Four hypotheses to investigate

Qualitative ranking only. None was fitted in this review; novelty across the entire fleet is unproven.

01 · medium cost · highest priority

Height-persistent cross-gradients

Compare orientation and displacement of magnetic and gravity gradient ridges across continuation heights; require hydrothermal corroboration. Could reveal buried steps without surface scarps. Mimics: unfaulted contacts and survey seams.

Uses restored TMI, RTP and gravity mirrors. HTCADS already uses updip projection and cross-gradients; multiscale persistence needs an explicit ablation.

02 · medium cost

Thermal direction uncertainty

Use GDR 2 m probes and well/spring chemistry with terrain, conductivity and structural orientation; test a fixed family of plausible transport directions rather than one upslope shift. Mimics: shallow groundwater flow, land-use anomalies and saline playas.

03 · low–medium cost

Visible-tip orientation agreement

Recompute visible-only tip geometry per fold and compare its orientation with potential-field and terrain lineaments. Mimics: drainage-aligned erosion and lithologic boundaries. Check prior implementations before claiming method novelty.

04 · high cost · data access unverified

Deformation–structure discordance

Test NASA OPERA DISP-S1 displacement gradients against GeoDAWN and 3DEP boundaries. Mimics: groundwater pumping and atmospheric/line-of-sight artifacts. NASA describes the product, but relevant tiles, access and coverage have not been verified here.
