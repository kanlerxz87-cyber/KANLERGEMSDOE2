Four testable geological hypotheses

Rank is qualitative research priority, not a numerical DTI forecast. Each remains unvalidated in this review; the top candidate was not fitted because the mandatory novelty preflight failed.

01 · highest priority · medium cost · existing inputs

Height-persistent potential-field crest migration

Layers: cached rtp, tmi_hg, iso_grav_anom, iso_grav_anom_hg, iso_grav_anom_vg, plus available upward-continuation products. Measure ridge orientation and crest displacement/persistence across heights, not simply a static gradient magnitude. A buried, dipping fault may shift the potential-field crest with continuation height and project its near-surface trace beneath basin fill.

Mimics: lithologic contacts, regional-field leveling seams, and magnetic remanence. Difference: explicit multi-height trajectory stability is an ablation of the frozen HT-CADS potential-field pillar; it must be tested separately, not assumed novel.

Uses restored owner-mirror core/GeoDAWN layers; byte identity is recorded, official-source provenance is not independently authenticated here.

02 · medium priority · medium cost · owner-mirror data present

Thermal transport-direction uncertainty

Layers: DOE GDR/INGENIOUS 2 m temperature probes and well/spring observations, cond_surf, terrain gradient, and structural ridge orientation. Compare a predeclared family of physically plausible up-gradient transport bearings and ask whether anomaly-to-structure convergence persists across bearings. This could target blind upflow offset from a mapped surface manifestation.

Mimics: shallow groundwater flow, seasonal land-surface temperature, pumping, and conductive playa sediments. Difference: sensitivity/uncertainty analysis rather than the lane's deterministic hydraulic back-projection.

The local owner mirror is listed present in the 2026-10-27 availability audit; confirm official GDR bytes and metadata before any model run. Official source: DOE GDR 1391 ↗.

03 · lower priority · low–medium cost · existing inputs

Hydrothermal alteration-edge / basement-step coincidence

Layers: GeoDAWN TMI, upward-continued magnetic data, radiometric K/Th (and relevant conductivity boundary), and gravity/magnetic cross-gradient. Test whether short-wavelength magnetic trough edges and radiometric alteration gradients coincide with a basement step, rather than scoring a broad thermal or alteration blob. Buried conduit margins may remain detectable where topographic scarps are absent.

Mimics: unfaulted lithologic contacts, alteration unrelated to active permeability, and survey seams. Difference: an edge-coincidence interaction test, not an unconstrained radial anomaly or single-layer alteration score.

GeoDAWN is an official USGS source; current local inputs are owner mirrors only. Source: USGS GeoDAWN ↗.

04 · exploratory only · high cost · not currently viable

Seasonal deformation barrier against structural conduits

Layers: NASA OPERA DISP-S1 time-series displacement gradients, cond_surf, and gravity-gradient structure. Seek repeatable cross-fault deformation discontinuities associated with buried hydraulic barriers, which could localize blind fault-fed circulation.

Mimics: pumping/irrigation, atmospheric delay, decorrelation, and line-of-sight geometry. Difference: temporal InSAR rather than static GPS strain. The specific competition-footprint tile and aligned time series are not present in the audited availability inventory, so this is not viable for validation now.

Free official source to check when access is available: NASA OPERA ↗. No claim that the needed tile has been downloaded or verified.
