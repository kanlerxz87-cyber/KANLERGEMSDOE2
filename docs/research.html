01 Highest qualitative priority · medium implementation effort

Thermal-path direction-uncertainty ensemble

Mechanism. Shallow probe and well/spring thermal anomalies can be laterally displaced from a deeper feeder by basin-fill transport. Instead of treating the smoothed terrain-elevation gradient as measured groundwater head, propagate each independent thermal source along a small, fixed set of terrain-gradient perturbations, then score only where paths meet independently supported structural ridges. The physical claim is a robustness test of the proxy, not a groundwater-flow inversion.

Exact layers. GDR #1391 2 m temperature-probe residuals and well/spring temperatures/geothermometers; `det_elev`, `cond_surf`, `grav_hg`, `rtp_hess_ridge_s15`, `base_depth_grad`, and `gallardo_meju_xgrad` from the supplied competition stack.

Blind-fault rationale & signature. A buried feeder may have no exposed scarp; independent probe and geothermometer sources should point toward a persistent gravity/magnetic/MT structural ridge. Predefine a directional-convergence score and require the thermal and structural components to remain individually sub-threshold outside that convergence. Use buffered whole-fault-segment holdout and exact visible-fault masking.

Mimics / controls. Shallow groundwater flow, irrigation or land-use heat, saline playa discharge, and topographic drainage can create terrain-following thermal patterns without geothermal faults. Negative controls: rotate/reverse transport directions and compare against non-geothermal GDR points; keep paleolake/tufa exclusions fixed.

What is already in this repo. `src/gems32/htcads.py` already back-projects 2 m probe and well/spring seeds along one terrain-elevation-gradient proxy and separately builds structural ridges. The proposed uncertainty ensemble and independent-component controls are not the same as that implementation; no claim is made about other repositories.

Data access. GDR #1391 is a public DOE repository record, listed as CC BY 4.0 in the dated source catalog (official record ↗, DOI 10.15121/1881483 ↗). The local manifest identifies owner-mirrored files, but `data/` and `.cache/` are absent here and no official bytes were re-fetched in this review. Competition layers are login-walled per the dated source record. Feasible after authorized restore; not now.

Expected-gain rank: 1 of 3 (qualitative). Expected numerical DTI: not estimated.

02 Medium qualitative priority · medium effort

Multi-field, depth-persistent crest migration

Mechanism. A dipping Basin-and-Range fault can place a deep-source horizontal gravity-gradient maximum basinward of its surface trace. Potential-field continuation should move or sharpen the crest systematically with source depth; matching gravity, magnetic/RTP, and basement-depth orientations can distinguish a structural step from a one-channel contact.

Exact layers. `iso_grav_anom` continued at predeclared 150/400/800 m; `rtp`, `tmi_vg`/GeoDAWN TMI and upward-continued TMI where available; `depth_to_base_surf`, `grav_hg`, and `base_flexure_curv`. Use existing 3DEP terrain only as a surface-contact control, not as the subsurface target.

Blind-fault rationale & signature. Require coherent displacement of potential-field crests across continuation heights, a gravity–magnetic cross-gradient orientation match, and an independently aligned basement-depth gradient; back-project along a preregistered dip range. A candidate fault should show a repeatable depth-ordered crest shift toward its basinward hanging wall, not just high edge strength.

Mimics / controls. Lithologic contacts, dikes, plutons, remanent magnetization, interpolation edges and survey seams can produce coincident gradients. Use lithology/contact controls, amplitude-preserving line shuffles, and hold out whole catalog fault segments; no same-segment neighbors enter training.

What is already in this repo. HTCADS computes an updip projection from one 400 m gravity continuation and a Gallardo–Meju cross-gradient; `src/gems32/dslup.py` already evaluates multilevel gravity continuation/strike coherence. The proposed joint migration signature across gravity, RTP/magnetics and basement depth is a distinct untested interaction in the inspected source tree, not a claim of fleet-wide novelty.

Data access. GeoDAWN’s official DOI is 10.5066/P93LGLVQ ↗; its dated source catalog lists the landing page as CC0. USGS 3DEP ↗ is listed as free and without use restrictions, but derived-tile coverage is not confirmed. Competition gravity/RTP/basement layers are described in the frozen manifest as owner mirrors, not independently organizer-authenticated; files are absent in this sandbox. No new external layer is required after authorized restore.

Expected-gain rank: 2 of 3 (qualitative). Expected numerical DTI: not estimated.

03 Lower qualitative priority · medium-to-high effort

Structural-ridge graph connectivity of independent thermal evidence

Mechanism. Blind faults may connect deep structural lineaments to separated surface thermal/geochemical manifestations. Represent independently detected gravity/magnetic/MT ridges as a graph and measure least-cost path/current-flow connectivity between separate GDR thermal sources; avoid scoring a hot spring merely because it is close to a known vent.

Exact layers. `struct_1d`, `grav_hess_ridge_s1`, `rtp_hess_ridge_s15`, `base_depth_grad`, `mt_claycap_edge`, GDR 2 m probes, well/spring temperatures and geothermometers, sinter/breccia/vent points, plus `det_elev` and `cond_surf` as transport/mimic controls.

Blind-fault rationale & signature. Require a path that links at least two independent thermal evidence classes to a subsurface structural ridge, remains after exact visible-fault masking, and persists when individual known vents are removed. Score the hidden conduit corridor rather than endpoint density alone.

Mimics / controls. Drainage networks, alluvial aquifers, lithologic contacts and clustered sampling can create apparent connectivity. Compare against degree-preserving randomized graphs, rotated ridge networks, and disconnected thermal endpoints; use only fold-visible catalogue geometry.

What is already in this repo. HTCADS currently smooths probe, well/spring and vent seeds into fields and combines evidence with ridge/conduit features; the inspected feature builder does not construct a graph or path-connectivity operator. This distinction is limited to this repository; no sister-site novelty claim is made.

Data access. The core GDR inputs are listed under official DOE GDR #1391 (record ↗), listed as CC BY 4.0 in the dated catalog. Owner-mirror file hashes are in `registry/data_manifest.json`; no actual data/cache directory exists in the workspace. Feasible after authorized restore; a fresh official download and provenance check remain necessary.

Expected-gain rank: 3 of 3 (qualitative). Expected numerical DTI: not estimated.

Code-review correction before any future HTCADS interpretation

Feature index 28 was labeled `anderson_slip_tendency` but computed cos(dom_strike_fp − 62°)². It has now been renamed strike_alignment_fixed_62deg; the pixel values are unchanged. It does not use a stress tensor, fault-plane normal, friction or a slip calculation. Further, the plume “upgradient” vector is derived from smoothed terrain elevation, not observed groundwater hydraulic head. Historical receipts retain their original names and must be read with this caveat.
