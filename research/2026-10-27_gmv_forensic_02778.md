# GEMSDOE32: a geometric explanation to test, not an established score mechanism

**Correction, 28 October 2026:** an earlier version incorrectly called predictions near the known-fault mask "pure false positives." DrivenData staff say the known-fault mask is **pixel-exact**. An unmasked prediction near a known fault is scored normally and can match a new hidden fault within the 300 m DTI radius. Proximity to a known fault does not, by itself, establish a false positive or reveal a score's cause. See the [staff clarification](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/4) and [competition metric](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/).

## What can be observed

The user's highest reported result among the nine focus sites is GEMSDOE32 **0.2778**, associated by the owner with H33-B2. It is **USER/OWNER-REPORTED**, not an organizer-confirmed score for the linked bytes: neither a file-specific organizer receipt nor hidden scoring labels were supplied. The frozen file is [`gemsdoe32-h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros.tif`](https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/GEMSDOE32/raw/1398109a23f04a2ace71c1bd2f527cfba87860b5/docs/downloads/gemsdoe32-h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros.tif), SHA-256 `c55bafc470054e8271dcb89347a17e07fefe50de6af6e6ba6c4b169ef7ab6fa9`.

The [SHA-pinned mirror comparison](../evidence/release_audit_20261028_independent.json) found:

* The GEMSDOE25 positive mask contains 44,090 pixels; GEMSDOE32 has 37,654. Removing the 6,436 GEMSDOE25 positives within an inclusive 2-pixel Euclidean distance of the **mirrored** known-fault mask reproduces the GEMSDOE32 positive mask exactly.
* GEMSDOE36 contains every GEMSDOE32 positive and 1,200 additional positives. The nine files are not spatially unique under the requested directional 3-pixel rule, despite differing byte hashes in many cases.
* The owner associates the higher score with this family of sparse, off-catalogue products. Thinning could reduce unhelpful predictions while retaining enough credit, **or** remove predictions that would have matched hidden faults. The hidden labels and score receipts needed to determine which happened are unavailable. Different linked versions, account results, and preprocessing also remain potential confounds.

Therefore we **cannot establish why** 0.2778 was highest or project a new score from this geometry. The public leaderboard demonstrates that values above 0.2778 have been observed by other participants; it does not establish that our lane or any specific file can exceed it. The appropriate test would be a pre-registered, buffered, segment-blocked holdout comparison under one evaluator with fold-visible-only catalogue features and a confidence interval—not a causal claim about distance alone.

## Release consequence

For the registered 37,654-dot HTCADS lane, a distinct lattice witness forces at least 31,005 dots into its 3-pixel neighborhood (82.341849%); excluding exact catalogue pixels strengthens the lower bound to 31,140 (82.700377%). Both exceed the required 70% maximum. This does **not** mean the witness is scientifically correct or its nearby dots are false positives; it is a counting-based **novelty stop** under the user's literal registry rule. See the [current audit](../evidence/release_audit_20261028_independent.json) and [current run card](../evidence/current_run_card.json). No new score or upload is authorized.
