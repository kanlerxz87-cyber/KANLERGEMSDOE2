## 3. PhD-level answer: why `0.2778` is the highest *reported* of the nine

**Short answer: precision surgery on a shared scarp manifold.** All nine files place dots on essentially the same
H19-5 ridge/scarp manifold (F4). Under a masked-Tversky score, dots within ~2 px of *known* faults are pure false
positives: the mask removes any truth they could match, while the `0.2·FP` term still taxes them. `GEMSDOE32` is the
only file that deletes exactly those dots (F2: −6,436), keeping every off-catalogue dot. Fewer dots + same recallable
mass ⇒ higher Tversky. The ladder corroborates the mechanism directionally: re-adding dots (`G36 = G32 + 1,200` →
`0.2750`) or keeping the near-catalogue dots (`G25` 44,090 → `0.2600`) both report lower.

**What is NOT proven:** which hidden pixels matched, how much of `+0.0178` over `G25` came from pruning vs. any other
byte difference, or that the mirrored bytes equal the submitted bytes. Rounded 4-decimal scores cannot identify hidden
truth counts (earlier inversion claims are retracted; the recomputed OLS constants live in
`evidence/ledger_recomputation_20261027.json` as **PROJECTIONs only**).

## 5. The `[0, 1]` portal error (user-reported)

`Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]` fires when any stored value is NaN, ±Inf, `< 0`, or `> 1` — most validators
compute `min/max`, and a single NaN poisons the comparison. Plausible cause of the user's rejection: submitting a
`-nan.tif` variant (NaN outside the irregular footprint, e.g. the `GEMSDOE25/28/31` mirrors) to a strict whole-array
check. The `-zeros.tif` artifacts in this repo clip to `[0, 1]`, store zeros outside, and pass the strict 13-point
audit (`gems32.submission.audit_geotiff`) with **zero human intervention**. Caveat: the official text says
null/NaN outside bounds (OS-1) while the sample mirror does the same — neither exterior encoding has an organizer
acceptance receipt here, so the promoted bundle keeps the zeros encoding that demonstrably passes the strict check,
and the validator receipt is published alongside the file.
