## Feasibility addendum (before any candidate experiment)

The literal registry contains the independently reopened spacing-5 calibration raster. Its <=3-pixel disks cover 5,160,724 of 5,167,373 footprint cells; only 6,649 cells are outside. For a 37,654-dot field, even selecting every uncovered cell forces at least 83.34% overlap. With the customary >2-pixel catalogue exclusion there are only 6,178 eligible uncovered cells, forcing at least 83.59% overlap. Both exceed the specified 70% stop. This is an exact cardinality bound, not a statistical score. The strongest conventional dot budget cannot pass; silently excluding the calibration raster would violate the prompt.

For a single **negative-feasibility scientific probe**, preselect at most 4,000 cells on the lattice-uncovered area, independent of any withheld catalogue labels, by the H1 multi-domain field; mask visible catalogue cells at evaluation. This is an explicit, severe registry-driven spatial constraint: it preferentially samples footprint edges and is not a credible geological prior or a proposed score improvement. It is recorded only to test whether a genuinely distinct research raster can exist under the literal rule, not to evade a failed gate by modifying a detected duplicate. Use no historic submission as the prediction surface. Compare the continuous sparse support before placement and the final dots against the full refreshed registry; stop on the first >70% or >0.90 violation. All catalogue-derived gates in a holdout must use visible faults only. Only after passing novelty and the leakage canaries may the shared evaluator run. The final selection, if any, is separate and no competition slot will be spent.

**Frozen implementation:** reuse the shared 35-channel stack, taking its RTP magnetic anomaly, isostatic gravity anomaly and detrended elevation. Extend each valid field by nearest valid value before filtering, to avoid artificial footprint-edge cliffs. At Gaussian sigma 2 pixels, use the RTP gradient as a local cross-strike normal; sample each field's directional Hessian at ±3 pixels along that normal. Each potential-field doublet feature is `max(0,-left*right)/(1+abs(left*right))`; multiply the geometric mean of magnetic/gravity doublets by squared magnetic/gravity gradient alignment and by `1/(1+0.01*|elevation gradient|)`. All constants are fixed before execution. These are uncalibrated engineering transforms, not physical probabilities. Four feature canaries plus the combined surface are evaluated individually on withheld positives versus sampled active, non-truth cells, per fold, as a leakage screen; if any two-sided AUC exceeds 0.90, stop. The 4,000-dot cap, deterministic tie break, lattice-uncovered support, and exact-pixel visible masking are fixed for this probe. The shared writer emits the research artifact only after both literal novelty checks pass. No catalogue-derived field or full-catalogue exclusion is used in the held-out score; the lattice is separately verified to be exactly the footprint-clipped every-fifth-pixel geometric grid, independent of fault labels.

## Post-run implementation disclosures

Review after the stopped run found that the implemented proxy used the **absolute** directional second derivatives and absolute gradient alignment. It did not enforce the preregistered signed-polarity pairing; the result is therefore not a complete test of the stated mechanism. In addition, the `masked_surface` canary used the production full-catalogue mask instead of a mask rebuilt from visible faults in each withheld fold. That canary is invalid as a fold-specific leakage test. The measured AUCs for the individual non-catalogue field/terrain features are separate diagnostics, not proof that a holdout pipeline is leakage-free. We did not redo the experiment, calculate holdout DTI, or change the duplicate-stop verdict.
